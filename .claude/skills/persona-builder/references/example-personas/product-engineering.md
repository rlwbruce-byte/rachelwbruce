# Persona: Product & Engineering (The Build-vs-Buy Decision Maker)

> Internal influencer. Frequently a blocker. Third persona in most deals.
> Last updated: April 2026

## Key titles
Head of Product, VP Product, CTO, Head of Engineering, ProductOps, Pricing Team, Engineering Manager

## Role in the buying process
Internal influencer and frequent blocker. Not usually the champion but must be won over. Build-first instinct — skeptical of vendor claims. Prefers peer-level conversations, not vendor pitches.

## Core pain points
- Engineering bandwidth consumed by non-core revenue logic (billing, rating, lifecycle management)
- Homegrown systems that became liabilities — technical debt compounds with every product or pricing change
- Tech debt from custom integrations — every upgrade or new model requires rework
- CPQ EOS forcing an architecture decision that lands on Engineering's plate
- Sprint velocity drained by billing infrastructure maintenance
- New pricing model blocked on roadmap because no one owns the billing layer

## What they want
- Engineering focused on the core product roadmap, not integration upkeep
- No new systems that create ongoing maintenance burden
- Quantified opportunity cost of building vs. buying
- Peer-level technical honesty about trade-offs — no vendor pitch
- Architecture that scales without custom code

## Metrics they own
- Sprint velocity
- Time-to-launch new pricing models (NPI Speed)
- Lines of custom code maintained
- NPI cycle time
- Integration uptime / reliability
- Tech debt ratio

## Messaging pillars (Product / Engineering lens)

**1. De-Risk Quote-to-Cash** — *"Don't build years of technical debt on day one."*
Every line of custom billing logic is future tech debt. Control eliminates that from the roadmap entirely — and doesn't require Engineering to own it post-launch.

**2. Scale Monetization** — *"Make pricing a business decision, not an engineering project."*
New pricing models (usage, credits, hybrid) shouldn't require getting on the roadmap. Control lets the business change how it sells without consuming Engineering sprint cycles.

**3. De-Risk Quote-to-Cash (build-vs-buy angle)** — *"Remove revenue infrastructure & pricing from the product roadmap."*
Quantify what homegrown billing infrastructure actually costs: sprint velocity, maintenance burden, upgrade fragility, and the compounding cost of every new model. Continuous buys that time back.

## Emotional driver
*Stop building what someone else already productized.*

## Buying triggers
- Sprint velocity drain from billing logic maintenance
- Homegrown system becoming a liability
- CPQ EOS forcing architecture decisions
- New pricing model blocked on roadmap
- CTO or Head of Eng asked to scope a billing infrastructure project

## Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We can build this ourselves." | The question isn't capability — it's opportunity cost. Every sprint spent on billing is a sprint not spent on the core product. What's the NPI cycle time for a new pricing model today vs. with Control? |
| "How much will this cost our team to own?" | Control is designed for Ops ownership post-launch — no custom code, no maintenance burden on Engineering. The cost is near zero to maintain vs. a homegrown system. |
| "How does this interact with our existing architecture?" | Control is embedded in Salesforce and NetSuite — it doesn't add a third system. It works within the architecture you already have. |
| "We're mid-implementation." | Control goes live without custom code and can layer into existing architecture without requiring reimplementation. |

## Email outreach notes
- **Tone:** Specific, technically honest, clear trade-offs. Quantify opportunity cost of building. Never pitch features — pitch productivity and architectural simplicity.
- **Length:** Under 150 words. Technical specificity beats marketing language. End with a peer-level conversation offer, not a "demo."
- **Cadence:** 5–7 business days between touches.

**Angle by title:**
- **CTO / Head of Engineering:** Architectural honesty — what does owning billing infrastructure actually cost? Frame Control as a buy decision that frees Engineering permanently.
- **VP Product / Head of Product:** NPI velocity. Every new pricing model shouldn't require a roadmap item. Control gives Product the ability to move independently of Engineering.
- **Pricing Team / ProductOps:** Day-to-day speed — configure and launch new models without dev tickets.
