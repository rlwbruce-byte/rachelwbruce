# Persona: Operations (The Practical Problem Solver)

> Primary champion and starting point for most deals.
> Last updated: April 2026

## Key titles
VP RevOps, RevOps Lead, Sales Ops, Order Management, BillingOps, Enterprise Applications, Salesforce Admin, Business Systems

## Role in the buying process
Primary champion and technical evaluator. First door in for most Control deals. Champions internally to Finance and IT.

## Core pain points
- Broken handoffs between Sales → Billing → Finance — the "messy middle" lives here
- Manual exceptions and workarounds (spreadsheets, Slack escalations, tribal knowledge)
- Can't operationalize complex pricing (usage, ramps, credits, hybrid) without custom dev or brittle middleware
- Every contract change creates cleanup — canceled orders, credit memos, rebilling cycles
- Cross-team dependency for every change — can't self-serve
- Escalation culture from both Sales and Finance simultaneously
- Integration fragility — middleware breaks on upgrades; custom scripts need ongoing maintenance
- SKU explosion and price list maintenance slows time-to-quote

## What they want
- Sales can move fast without breaking NetSuite
- Lifecycle changes (amendments, renewals, upsells, cancels) work without manual cleanup
- Fewer escalations, fewer exceptions
- Shorter quote-to-cash cycle with fewer manual touches
- New revenue models without a systems project
- No "forever contractors" just to keep the integration running

## Metrics they own
- Quote-to-Cash Cycle Time
- Manual Touches per Order
- Exception Rates
- Quote-to-Order Accuracy
- Time to Bill / Daily Unbilled
- NPI Speed (New Product Introduction)
- Escalation Volume
- Integration Uptime

## Messaging pillars (Operations lens)

**1. De-Risk Quote-to-Cash** — *"Don't build years of technical debt on day one."*
Go live without embedding custom logic into integrations or creating cross-team dependencies. Ops doesn't inherit a brittle system that requires babysitting.

**2. Eliminate the Messy Middle** — *"Don't let your workarounds become the system."*
Contract changes flow automatically into the correct NetSuite sales orders. No cancel-and-rebill. No spreadsheet bridges. No escalation queue.

**3. Scale Monetization** — *"Make growth a business decision, not a systems negotiation."*
Launch usage, ramp, credit, and hybrid models out-of-the-box in minutes, not months. Ops doesn't become the bottleneck every time the business changes how it sells.

**4. CPQ EOS (Operations angle)** — *"Extending today, accelerating tomorrow."*
Control extends CPQ's life with modern pricing models right now, while setting up the architecture for RCA/ARM on their timeline — without Ops absorbing a forced rip-and-replace.

## Emotional driver
*Stop absorbing chaos. Own the process.*

## Buying triggers
- Contract change that breaks downstream billing
- New pricing model that can't be operationalized without a systems project
- CPQ EOS urgency forcing an architecture decision
- Sprint velocity drain from building/maintaining billing logic internally

## Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We use Boomi / MuleSoft / Workato." | Middleware moves data — it doesn't understand contract lifecycle. Every amendment still creates manual cleanup. Control replaces the middleware layer with lifecycle-aware logic built in. |
| "IT owns this, not us." | Ops absorbs every failure. Control reduces IT dependency by design — no custom code, no ongoing maintenance burden. |
| "We'll build this internally." | Building means Ops waits on the product roadmap or IT backlog. Control lets Ops configure and launch without a dev ticket. |
| "We're mid-implementation." | Control goes live without custom code. It can layer into an existing architecture without reimplementation. |
| "CPQ EOS concerns." | Control extends CPQ's life and works with RCA too — no forced platform choice. EOS is not EOL — you have time. |

- **Length:** Under 150 words. No jargon. Asset embedded in body. End with a meeting/demo request.
- **Cadence:** 5–7 business days between touches.
