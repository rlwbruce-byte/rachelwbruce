Fiddler's own site never names "CISO" directly — this persona is synthesized from its published Security & Trust, Approver, and Executive-buyer roles plus governance-heavy site messaging. Flagged here, not presented as on-site language.

Persona Overview

Role in the buying process: Approver / risk-owning executive — not the initial champion (that's Head of AI/ML), but a required sign-off gate for the Governance/GRC play, and pulled in early on the Guardrails/Trust play once data-leakage or jailbreak exposure is on the table.

Key titles: CISO, VP Security, Head of Security & Trust, Chief Risk Officer (regulated-industry variant), Chief AI Officer (where security and AI risk ownership are combined)

ICP context: Enterprise, regulated-industry-heavy — Fiddler's named base skews financial services, insurance, and public sector (Mastercard, Ally, American Family, Elevance Health, DTCC, U.S. Navy) — exactly where a CISO holds hard sign-off authority over production AI/agent traffic.

What They Care About

Core Pain Points

  • Production LLM/agent apps create an ungoverned attack surface — prompt injection, jailbreaks, PII/PHI leakage — existing AppSec tooling wasn't built to see.
  • Passive monitoring isn't enough: detecting a leak after it happened doesn't satisfy a prevention mandate.
  • Regulatory mapping is fragmented — EU AI Act, HIPAA, SR 11-7, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, NAIC each demand different evidence.
  • Vendor sprawl — guardrail scanners, red-team tools, and prompt-firewalls each cover a slice, none give one governed control point.
  • No clean audit trail from "what did the agent do" to "what can we prove to a regulator."
  • Security is structurally a step behind fast-moving AI/ML engineering teams shipping agents.

Desired Outcomes

  • Inline, preventative enforcement on every production agent's request/response path.
  • One auditable governance layer mapped to frameworks that already apply to the business.
  • One control plane instead of a patchwork of point security tools.
  • Enforcement that doesn't cost latency or block engineering velocity.
  • Provable, exportable audit/incident-response evidence on demand.

Metrics That Matter

Guardrail/enforcement latency (<80ms claimed) · Jailbreak-block precision / toxic-content detection rate · Time to produce an audit trail · Incident response time for AI-related events · Number of point tools consolidated · % of production traffic actually enforced (not just monitored)

Messaging Pillars (CISO Lens)

Prevention over passive evaluation — "The system of trust for your agents."

Inline, enforcing controls on the request/response path — blocking harmful outputs and PII/PHI/secrets exposure before they leave the system, not a dashboard that tells you after the fact.

Auditable governance, mapped to real regulation — "Protect from risks. Maximize ROI."

Explicit mapping to NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, HIPAA, SR 11-7, NAIC, and the EU AI Act, plus a continuous audit trail — turns "we have an AI governance program" into something handed directly to an auditor.

Persona-Specific Nuance

A CISO is rarely the deal's starting point — Head of AI/ML or a Platform/MLOps champion typically initiates, with Security pulled in once the app is headed toward production or a compliance deadline forces the question. Lead with the prevention-vs-monitoring distinction and named regulatory frameworks before any category language — generic "AI governance" pitches get filtered out fast by a buyer who reviews vendor security claims for a living.
